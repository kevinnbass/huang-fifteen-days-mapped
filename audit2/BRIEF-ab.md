## The figure's rules you are checking the rows against
- A chip is one dated act, worded from a saved report. Words inside double quotation marks must be in the source as quoted (an ellipsis or square brackets mark a cut or an insertion).
- An act sits on the day its source gives: a day the source states, or one its context fixes. Failing that it sits on the day the source was first published, and then the chip has a dashed left edge ("the act itself may be earlier"). In `rows.jsonl` and `public/research/nv8_rows.tsv` such a row has `reported_by_others` true.
- A passage is dated by the earliest saved version that carries it. Where only one version of a changed page is saved, the chip keeps the day of FIRST publication and says the passage "may be newer". Where an earlier version is saved and lacks the passage, the passage sits on the day of the version that has it. Where the only saved copy of a report was updated later, a relay of the report's first day that carries the sentence shows that the sentence was in it. A relay is dated by its own page. A page's day in `public/research/nv8_articles.tsv` is a U.S. Eastern day.
- Two acts of one actor on one day share a chip; where only one of the two is dated by its report, the edge is solid and the words say which is the report.
- Where a chip rests on one outlet's account of another's report, it says so. Each reported exchange carries the denial its own source reports.
- No motive, no characterisation of people; public roles only. A private person is named by role.
- Refusals: an explicit refusal by a drawn actor to comment on its own conduct or part in the matter is drawn once for each documented response, with the answer the same source prints beside it; a report that no reply had come to a request, or that someone "did not say", is not a refusal; a department's statements and refusals are drawn on its secretary's row.
- An act is drawn when a saved report gives it, it is on the matter and no chip of that actor already carries the same occurrence. Identity of occurrence needs proof (the same post, the same document, the same occasion), not a similar view. An act found to duplicate another is withdrawn: its row stays in the table with the reason and is not drawn.
- A part of a statement already drawn is added when it is one of four things: an answer about the actor's own conduct, a hedge or a denial, the main message of a statement whose chip quoted a lesser phrase, or a concrete act. A reason given for a position already drawn is not added.
- **Reports of earlier acts**: a report published in these days about something an actor did earlier, on the matter, is drawn on the day of the FIRST saved page of these days that carries it, with words that say whose report it is and that the act is earlier. Where the reporting paper has its own row (the Times, the Journal, the FT), the report is drawn on the PAPER's row; where the outlet has no row, on the row of the actor it is about, with a dashed edge. "The matter" includes the succession. **Not applied to everything, and said so:** the census showed that this rule covers several hundred recalled acts; the figure draws those that earlier readings and the builder's corrections reached, and lists the rest as open in the register.
- Chips cite pages inside the fifteen days, or an actor's own original or a reference document outside the table of pages. No chip cites a page of the coverage dated after Nov 12.
- Besides chips, the figure holds earlier facts as TIE LINES between two drawn parties (rows of `public/research/nv8_rows.tsv` whose table is not "dated act of an actor"; kinds `money`, `context` and others; a `context` line from a party to itself is worded in its row with no line drawn). An earlier act already drawn as a tie line is not drawn again as a chip.
- Look-ups: an analyst, expert or byline is drawn only with a tie on record. At a university an award counts for a person only where the record names that person's own unit. Two people of one name are one person only on proof.
- **Records about Anthropic and OpenAI.** Since Nov 17 the builder draws facts about the positions and ties of Anthropic and of OpenAI as soon as it finds them, without waiting for the owner's decision on recalled earlier acts, and says so in the status (its reason: it is Anthropic's model). Nothing else in the register is drawn on purpose until that decision.
- **Days abroad.** An act done abroad sits on the local day its source names, and its chip then gives both clocks; a post or a page stamp is read as a U.S. Eastern day. The builder states this rule and asks whether it is even-handed.
- **Unanswered requests.** A report that a request for comment went unanswered ("did not respond", "could not be reached") is not drawn; an explicit refusal ("declined to comment") is, once for each documented response, saying who asked and about what.

## How to mark each finding (as in the last eight readings)
Begin the `problem` cell of every line that is not `ok`, in every findings file, with exactly one of these marks, and count them in the report:
- `[row error]` something a drawn row says is wrong against its source: words not in the source or not that speaker's, a wrong day or edge, a wrong or missing relay, a paraphrase that says more, less, harsher or gentler than the source, or a chip that without an omitted denial, hedge or answer misstates what the actor said.
- `[missing occasion]` a separate act on the matter by a drawn actor that no chip or tie line carries AND that the register does not list.
- `[missing part]` a further part of a statement or occasion that a chip already carries, which you would add under the four tests and whose absence does not make the chip misstate the actor. Name the test it meets.
- `[table or text]` a count, a table cell (a disposition in the census table, the register, the table of the nine pages' records or a look-up included), or a sentence of the note, the status or the README that is wrong or says too much.
- `[gauge]` a label or a gauge reading that is wrong with no act behind it.
Where you hesitate between `[row error]` and `[missing part]`, ask whether a reader of the chip alone would come away with a wrong idea of what the actor said or did; say in a few words why you chose.
