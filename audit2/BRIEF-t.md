## The figure's rules you are checking the rows against
- A chip is one dated act, worded from a saved report. Words inside double quotation marks must be in the source as quoted (an ellipsis or square brackets mark a cut or an insertion).
- An act sits on the day its source gives: a day the source states, or one its context fixes (a remark dated with others like it; an answer to something that happened that day in a report of that day). Failing that it sits on the day the source was first published, and then the chip has a dashed left edge ("the act itself may be earlier"). Where a cited page was changed after it was first published, a passage taken from it may be newer than the act's day, and the note says for how many acts. In `rows.jsonl` and `public/research/nv8_rows.tsv` such a row has `reported_by_others` true.
- Two acts of one actor on one day share a chip; where only one of the two is dated by its report, the edge is solid and the words say which is the report.
- Where a chip rests on one outlet's account of another's report, it says so. Each reported exchange carries the denial its own source reports.
- No motive, no characterisation of people; public roles only.
- Refusals: an explicit refusal by a drawn actor to comment on its own conduct or part in the matter is drawn once for each documented response, with the answer the same source prints beside it; refusals of one day to several outlets share one act that names each; a report that no reply had come to a request is not a refusal and is drawn for no one; a department's statements and refusals are drawn on its secretary's row.
- An act is drawn when a saved report gives it, it is on the matter and no chip of that actor already carries the same occurrence: a similar remark on another occasion is its own act; a later article repeating a dated remark is not. Identity of occurrence needs proof (the same post, the same document, the same occasion), not a similar view.
- A part of a statement already drawn is added as an act of the same day when it is one of four things: an answer about the actor's own conduct, a hedge or a denial, the main message of a statement whose chip quoted a lesser phrase, or a concrete act. A reason given for a position already drawn is not added.
- A chief or chairman of a company that is already drawn can supply an act on that company's row, and the chip says in what role he spoke; a chief quoted once gets no row of his own.
- A report published in these days about something the actor did earlier is drawn on the report's day, with words that say whose report it is and that the act is earlier.

## How to mark each finding (as in the eighteenth reading)
Begin the `problem` cell of every line that is not `ok`, in every findings file, with exactly one of these marks, and count them in the report:
- `[row error]` something a drawn row says is wrong against its source: words not in the source or not that speaker's, a wrong day or edge, a wrong or missing relay, a paraphrase that says more, less, harsher or gentler than the source, or a chip that without an omitted denial, hedge or answer misstates what the actor said.
- `[missing occasion]` a separate act on the matter by a drawn actor (another occasion, document or response) that no chip carries.
- `[missing part]` a further part of a statement or occasion that a chip already carries, which you would add under the four tests and whose absence does not make the chip misstate the actor. Name the test it meets.
- `[table or text]` a count, a table cell, or a sentence of the note, the status or the README that is wrong or says too much.
- `[gauge]` a label or a gauge reading that is wrong with no act behind it.
Where you hesitate between `[row error]` and `[missing part]`, ask whether a reader of the chip alone would come away with a wrong idea of what the actor said or did; say in a few words why you chose.
