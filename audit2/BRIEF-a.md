# Second independent audit of a public figure, part one of three: "Fifteen days, mapped" — every new or changed row against its saved original, and the figure element by element

Owner: the session that built the figure. You are the auditor. You did not build this figure. Work only inside audit2; write only under audit2/out/. Change nothing else anywhere. Do not fetch anything from the web.

The figure ("Fifteen days, mapped: the coverage, who acted, and the documented ties") covers Oct 29 to Nov 12, 2026, the fortnight in which reports about Nvidia's founder, Jensen Huang, led to his resignation as chief executive. It shows one dot per piece of coverage that two news aggregators listed; one row per actor (newspapers, reporters, analysts, 24 members of Congress, the administration, Nvidia, others) with a chip for each dated act; and documented ties between those actors and the AI company Anthropic, its people and groups it or Open Philanthropy has funded. It is public (the copy in `public/` is the repository as pushed). A first audit read it on Nov 14; since then it was changed a great deal by its builder and **nothing changed since has been read by anyone but the builder**. The builder is a model made by Anthropic, so read with that in mind: look as hard for an understated or missing fact about Anthropic as for an overstated one, and check that other companies (Nvidia above all) are described with the same care. The figure's own rule is that a tie is a dated record, not a motive.

Two other auditors work on the other two parts in their own folders at the same time (part two recounts the coverage and the money; part three tests who and what was chosen). Do your own part in full; do not wait for them or read their folders.

## The one hard rule about names
Some originals hold the names of private people: employees who gave money, and lobbyists. **Never write the name of a donor or of a lobbyist in any file you produce.** Refer to an FEC line by its SUB_ID or by committee, date and amount. Members of Congress, companies, organisations, firms, political committees, officials, and reporters, analysts and other speakers as the saved news pages name them may be named.

## Part A: every new or changed row that is not a listed piece (168 rows) — read each one
For each row of `delta.tsv` whose `table` is not `piece listed by an aggregator`, take the row as it stands from `rows.jsonl`, open every source it cites and read enough to judge the whole row (not only the changed words); do not rely on `q` alone, and never take the row's own wording as evidence. Append one line to `out/findings.csv` (header `finding_id,row_id,grade,passage_found,problem,suggested_wording`):
- `grade`: `ok` (the sources support everything `drawn_text` says), `qualification` (supported, but the wording goes further than the source, drops a limit the source states, gives a date or an amount the source does not give, or leaves a reader with a wrong impression), or `error` (a fact in `drawn_text` is not in the sources or contradicts them).
- `passage_found`: the words in the source that decide your grade, at most 300 characters (never a donor's or lobbyist's name).
- `problem` and `suggested_wording` for `qualification` and `error` only; the suggestion must be something the sources support.
Read these most closely:
- the 75 new or changed rows of `dated act of an actor`. Is the act dated as the source dates it (the day the thing was said or done, not the day a page reported it), attributed to the right actor, and worded as the source words it? Where a chip puts words in quotation marks, are they the source's words exactly, and the speaker's own rather than the page's paraphrase? Where a chip restates one outlet's account of another's report ("As CNBC relays it"), is that clear? Where one chip carries two remarks of one day, is each part supported by a source for that day? Is a denial, a limit or a later correction in the same source left out?
- chips that cite an original beside the report that relays it (`private/originals_added.tsv`, 23 passages on 20 rows): is each passage in the saved original, and does the original say what the chip says, on the day the chip gives? Two chips were moved to the day their original carries (Sen. Warren's statement, to Oct 29; the shareholders' complaint, to Nov 9): is each new day right, and does any other chip still carry a relay's day where its own original shows another?
- one act is drawn without a day ("Before these days", a senator's letter of Oct 2025): is it plain to a reader that it lies outside the fortnight, and is everything said of it in the sources?
- the 61 new or changed rows of `tie of a member of Congress`: recompute every amount, count of people and date range from `public/research/employee_gifts.csv` and `public/research/anthropac_by_recipient.csv`; where a line says "$X of it to a joint committee", recompute X from the lines whose committee is in `public/research/joint_committees.csv`. For lines about lobbyists' gifts, lobbying reports, fellows, advisers, former staff and witnesses, open the pack rows. A witness list does not show who appeared; a lobbyist's gift is the lobbyist's own money; "no gift on file" must be bounded to the records and the dates searched.
- the 14 new or changed rows of `tie between two actors` (each named link, each post and its dates, each grant total and its years), the 13 new notes, the 3 changed backgrounds and the 2 counts across Congress.

## Part B: the figure, element by element — read all of it
The figure was redrawn in three slices side by side, and its section notes and its long footnote were largely rewritten. Read all visible text of `public/figures/nv8-fifteen-days-mapped.html` (title, subtitle, the tiles, legend, the day axis and its counts, every actor's row and chip, every line of each member's ties, the network at left, section notes, every sentence of the footnote). Append to `out/figure_findings.csv` (header `finding_id,grade,element_text,problem,rows_checked`) one line per problem, and a closing line `B-CLOSE` saying how many elements you read:
- a visible sentence or number that no listed row or table supports: recount every count the figure states that you can recount from `rows.jsonl` and `public/research/` (the pieces and sites, the pieces per day, the actors and chips drawn, the members who received a gift from an Anthropic employee and from AnthroPAC, each "named on N of the 1,086 saved pages", the party counts, the totals across the 535 members, the dates the money was read through and the date the next reports are due);
- a row id or a file name visible to a reader;
- wording that states or implies motive, coordination or cause; a line drawn from a tie to an act rather than to a person or body;
- a place where the figure should say what the record does not show and does not; a negative stated more widely than the search behind it;
- a tie, an actor or an act that a reader new to the subject could not follow from the figure alone, or would misread; an actor drawn in more than one slice in a way that could be read as two actors;
- something in a row's sources that weakens or contradicts what is drawn and is left out;

## Rules
- Read, do not script, wherever this brief says read: a judgement of a row, a remark or a reason comes from opening the source and reading it. Scripts are for counts, recounts and searches, and every script you write stays in `out/`.
- A negative ("none found", "no line", "not in the table") counts only with a control that shows your check could have seen a hit; the control must be something that is in the files you searched. Record each control and its result.
- A missing file, an unreadable original, a page the builder could not save is a gap, not a finding and not a negative: write it to `out/gaps.csv` (`row_id_or_key,source,what_failed`) and go on. Never grade from memory or outside knowledge.
- Be exact and plain. No guesses about why anyone did anything. Grade down wording that states or implies a motive, describes a person's character, goes beyond a public role, or implies that one event caused another when no source says so.
- Append only; if an output file already has a line for a key, skip it (this lane may be restarted). Keep a short `out/ROUNDS.md` note at the end of the round: what was done, what failed, what a next round must check first.
- Before you finish, search every file under `out/` for the names of donors and lobbyists you met in the originals and remove any you find; say in the report that you did.
