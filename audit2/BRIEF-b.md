# Second independent audit of a public figure, part two of three: "Fifteen days, mapped" — the coverage and the money, recounted from the saved records

Owner: the session that built the figure. You are the auditor. You did not build this figure. Work only inside audit2; write only under audit2/out/. Change nothing else anywhere. Do not fetch anything from the web.

The figure ("Fifteen days, mapped: the coverage, who acted, and the documented ties") covers Oct 29 to Nov 12, 2026, the fortnight in which reports about Nvidia's founder, Jensen Huang, led to his resignation as chief executive. It shows one dot per piece of coverage that two news aggregators listed; one row per actor (newspapers, reporters, analysts, 24 members of Congress, the administration, Nvidia, others) with a chip for each dated act; and documented ties between those actors and the AI company Anthropic, its people and groups it or Open Philanthropy has funded. It is public (the copy in `public/` is the repository as pushed). A first audit read it on Nov 14; since then it was changed a great deal by its builder and **nothing changed since has been read by anyone but the builder**. The builder is a model made by Anthropic, so read with that in mind: look as hard for an understated or missing fact about Anthropic as for an overstated one, and check that other companies (Nvidia above all) are described with the same care. The figure's own rule is that a tie is a dated record, not a motive.

Two other auditors work on the other two parts in their own folders at the same time (part one reads every changed row and the figure; part three tests who and what was chosen). Do your own part in full; do not wait for them or read their folders.

## The one hard rule about names
Some originals hold the names of private people: employees who gave money, and lobbyists. **Never write the name of a donor or of a lobbyist in any file you produce.** Refer to an FEC line by its SUB_ID or by committee, date and amount. Members of Congress, companies, organisations, firms, political committees, officials, and reporters, analysts and other speakers as the saved news pages name them may be named.

## Part A: the coverage, recounted (a census, not a sample)
`rows.jsonl` has 2,225 rows of `piece listed by an aggregator` (the first audit saw 1,662; 896 are new or changed), each citing one saved aggregator page and the headline as listed. Write your own script (keep it in `out/`) and report in `out/census.md`:
1. For every such row: is the headline (the passage) in the saved page it cites? Is the day first listed the earliest saved page that lists that address in `public/research/harvest.tsv`, and is the timed capture named for it in `public/research/coverage_day_pages.tsv` a capture of that day? Count failures and list up to 30 by row id.
2. Recount from `public/research/harvest.tsv`, `coverage_rows.tsv` and `coverage_scope.tsv`: the links harvested, the pieces on the story, the wider fallout and the others, the number of sites, the pieces per day, the pieces with no day; compare with every number the figure prints. Read the word rule in `public/scripts/story_scope.py` and say whether it is applied as written; then read (do not script) 150 pieces it kept and 150 it left out, drawn by a seeded random choice you record, and say how many of each are plainly wrong, with the addresses.
3. Whether every saved aggregator page in the range is cited by at least one row, and which hours or days have no saved page (a gap in the harvest, not a day without coverage); whether the last day's column is cut off by the time of the last saved page, and whether the figure says so.
4. `public/research/story_pages.tsv` and `member_selection.tsv`: recount, for every name searched, the pages that name it, and compare with what the figure prints in each member's row; say whether any current member named on three or more saved pages is neither drawn nor listed with a reason.

## Part B: the money, recounted from the FEC's records
The figure counts a gift for a member when an FEC line gives Anthropic (or Nvidia) as the donor's employer and its committee is tied to the member: the member's campaign committee, a leadership PAC the member sponsors, or a joint fundraising committee whose statement of organisation lists that member's committee and no other sitting member's. The joint committees were added on Nov 14 after the builder found it had missed them; this is the part of the figure most likely to be wrong, in either direction.
Saved records (under `research/primary/`): `fec-bulk-indiv26-employers-20261113` (lines of the FEC's bulk individual-contributions file for the two employers), `fec-sched_a-employer-anthropic-2026`, `fec-sched_a-employer-nvidia-2026` and the two `...-since-2026-10-01-20261114` folders (the same from the FEC's API, and lines dated from Oct 1), `fec-bulk-indiv26-banks-amended-20261113`, `fec-bulk-committees-2026-20261113` (the FEC's committee master and its committee-to-candidate links), `fec-jfc-<committee id>-20261114` (67 folders: a joint committee's or leadership PAC's record and its statement of organisation, `f1-*.fec`), `fec-committee-<committee id>-20261114` (42 folders), `fec-bulk-weball26-20261113`, `fec-reporting-dates-2026-20261114` (the FEC's reporting calendar), `lake-bioguide-fec-xref-20261113`. The 535 sitting members and their FEC candidate ids: `EVIDENCE_SETS/congress/research/members_network.csv` (`cohort` = sitting). These files hold donors' names: the hard rule applies.
Write your own scripts and report in `out/money.md`, with a table for each point:
1. Recompute `public/research/joint_committees.csv` from the saved statements of organisation and records, without using the builder's `private/jfc_participants.csv` and `lpac_sponsors.csv` except to compare at the end. For every committee: its participants as the statement lists them, which of them is a sitting member's campaign committee or a leadership PAC sponsored by a sitting member (a leadership PAC belongs to its sponsor), and the outcome under the figure's rule. List every difference from the published table. Look hardest for a second sitting member among the participants of a committee the table ties to one member (a member running for another office, a participant listed under an older committee name, a leadership PAC of another member), and for a committee attributed to no one that the rule would tie to one member.
2. From the raw lines, not from the builder's table: every line that gives either employer, by the kind of committee that received it (a sitting member's campaign committee, a leadership PAC, a joint committee by outcome, a party committee, a PAC, a conduit such as ActBlue or WinRed, a candidate who is not a sitting member, other), with lines and dollars for each employer. Which kinds does the figure count, which not, and does it say so? Is any committee with the FEC designation of a joint fundraising committee that received such a line missing from the published table?
3. Recompute `public/research/employee_gifts.csv` (lines, dollars, donors numbered, per member) and compare: the totals across the 535 members that the figure prints for each company (members, dollars), and each of the 24 drawn members' lines (dollars, people, first and last date, the part given to a joint committee). Are amended reports counted once? Is one donor counted once per member? Are the two employers matched by the same rule (list the employer strings counted for each, and strings in the saved lines that look like the same employer and are not counted)? Are refunds or negative lines handled the same way for both?
4. The sentences about October: that the lines are read through a stated date; that no line of a sitting member's campaign committee dated after Sep 30, 2026 is on file; the number and dollars of conduit lines dated Oct 1 to 14 for each company; that the reports covering the fifteen days are due on Dec 3, 2026 (open the saved calendar). Recount each and compare with the footnote and with `public/research/employee_gift_rates.csv`.
5. AnthroPAC: recount `public/research/anthropac_by_recipient.csv` against the saved filings it cites for the 24 drawn members, and the figure's count of drawn members who received an AnthroPAC contribution.
6. Say plainly whether, after your recount, any drawn member's line overstates or understates what the records show, and whether Nvidia's side is counted less completely than Anthropic's anywhere the figure does not say so.
Append every difference to `out/money_findings.csv` (header `finding_id,grade,where,what_the_figure_says,what_the_records_show,suggested_wording`), grade `error` or `qualification`.

## Rules
- Read, do not script, wherever this brief says read: a judgement of a row, a remark or a reason comes from opening the source and reading it. Scripts are for counts, recounts and searches, and every script you write stays in `out/`.
- A negative ("none found", "no line", "not in the table") counts only with a control that shows your check could have seen a hit; the control must be something that is in the files you searched. Record each control and its result.
- A missing file, an unreadable original, a page the builder could not save is a gap, not a finding and not a negative: write it to `out/gaps.csv` (`row_id_or_key,source,what_failed`) and go on. Never grade from memory or outside knowledge.
- Be exact and plain. No guesses about why anyone did anything. Grade down wording that states or implies a motive, describes a person's character, goes beyond a public role, or implies that one event caused another when no source says so.
- Append only; if an output file already has a line for a key, skip it (this lane may be restarted). Keep a short `out/ROUNDS.md` note at the end of the round: what was done, what failed, what a next round must check first.
- Before you finish, search every file under `out/` for the names of donors and lobbyists you met in the originals and remove any you find; say in the report that you did.
