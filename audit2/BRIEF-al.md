## The figure's rules you are checking the rows against
- A chip is one dated act, worded from a saved report. Words inside double quotation marks must be in the source as quoted (an ellipsis or square brackets mark a cut or an insertion).
- An act sits on the day its source gives: a day the source states, or one its context fixes. Failing that it sits on the day the source was first published, and then the chip has a dashed left edge ("the act itself may be earlier"). In `rows.jsonl` and `public/research/nv8_rows.tsv` such a row has `reported_by_others` true.
- A passage is dated by the earliest saved version that carries it. Where only one version of a changed page is saved, the chip keeps the day of FIRST publication and says the passage "may be newer". Where an earlier version is saved and lacks the passage, the passage sits on the day of the version that has it. Where the only saved copy of a report was updated later, a relay of the report's first day that carries the sentence shows that the sentence was in it. A relay is dated by its own page. A page's day in `public/research/nv8_articles.tsv` is a U.S. Eastern day.
- Two acts of one actor on one day share a chip; where only one of the two is dated by its report, the edge is solid and the words say which is the report.
- Where a chip rests on one outlet's account of another's report, it says so. Each reported exchange carries the denial its own source reports.
- No motive, no characterisation of people; public roles only. A private person is named by role. **Stated since the answer to the thirty-fifth reading** (README, `Who made this`): the figure describes no one in its own words beyond a public role, and attributed words about a person stay when they are themselves the record, each with its speaker. - Refusals: an explicit refusal by a drawn actor to comment on its own conduct or part in the matter is drawn once for each documented response, with the answer the same source prints beside it; a report that no reply had come to a request, or that someone "did not say", is not a refusal; a department's statements and refusals are drawn on its secretary's row. **Wider for Anthropic and OpenAI since the answer to the thirty-third reading (the owner has still to decide whether it should hold for every actor):** a refusal of either company to comment on any record that the test for the two companies covers (its money included) is drawn too, as a chip when it falls inside the fifteen days and on the company's line otherwise. ## How to mark each finding (as in the earlier readings)
Begin the `problem` cell of every line that is not `ok`, in every findings file, with exactly one of these marks, and count them in the report:
- `[row error]` something a drawn row says is wrong against its source: words not in the source or not that speaker's, a wrong day or edge, a wrong or missing relay, a paraphrase that says more, less, harsher or gentler than the source, or a chip that without an omitted denial, hedge or answer misstates what the actor said.
- `[missing occasion]` a separate act on the matter by a drawn actor that no chip or tie line carries AND that the register does not list.
- `[missing part]` a further part of a statement or occasion that a chip already carries, which you would add under the four tests and whose absence does not make the chip misstate the actor. Name the test it meets.
- `[table or text]` a count, a table cell (a disposition in the census table, the register, the table of the nine pages' records or a look-up included), or a sentence of the note, the status or the README that is wrong or says too much.
- `[gauge]` a label or a gauge reading that is wrong with no act behind it.
Where you hesitate between `[row error]` and `[missing part]`, ask whether a reader of the chip alone would come away with a wrong idea of what the actor said or did; say in a few words why you chose.
