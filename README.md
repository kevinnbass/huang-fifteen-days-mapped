# Fifteen days, mapped: the coverage of the Huang affair, who acted, and the documented ties

**Status: draft.** Every quoted passage has been matched by script to a saved copy of its source, and the counts in the
figure are recomputed from the tables here by `scripts/verify_nv8.py`. A tie is a dated record, not a motive. Nothing
here says why anyone acted.

## What this is

On Oct 29, 2026 The New York Times reported that U.S. officials saw Jensen Huang, the founder and then chief executive of
Nvidia, as the target of a Chinese spy plot. Fifteen days later he had resigned as chief executive and three House
committees had called him to answer questions. This figure puts three things on one sheet for those fifteen days:

- **The coverage.** One dot for each of the 2,245 pieces that two news aggregators, Techmeme and Memeorandum, listed
  on the story, on the first day a saved page lists it (2,254 pieces from 485 sites in all; 9 seen only on undated pages are
  not drawn).
- **Who acted.** One row for each actor (newspapers, reporters, analysts, 27 members of Congress, the administration,
  Nvidia, others) and one chip for each dated act: 165 acts, each worded from a saved report.
- **The documented ties.** At the left, the records that tie those actors to a company, a funder or a group: the AI
  company Anthropic, its people and its PAC; groups that Anthropic or the foundation Open Philanthropy has funded; and
  Nvidia itself. The ties are mapped outward from Anthropic and Open Philanthropy; Nvidia's are added where the same
  kind of record was searched, and the figure's note lists what was searched for it and what was not found. There are 150 lines for the 27 members of Congress (gifts by each company's
  employees, lobbying reports that name a member's bill, fellows and former staff, lobbyists' gifts, ads), a line on
  AnthroPAC's contribution for each member who received one, and 84 records among the other actors.

Why those parties: the matter touches the export of the AI chips that Nvidia sells. On the record here: Anthropic backs
"smart export controls on AI chips" and gave $40 million to the group Public First Action; members of Congress drawn
here sponsor bills on chip exports (one, the AI OVERWATCH Act, would let Congress block advanced chip exports); both
Anthropic's and Nvidia's lobbying reports name those bills; AMD and Broadcom compete with Nvidia for AI chip sales. The
figure's own boxes say, for each group of actors, what is on the record about it and what no document here shows.

A line ends at a person or a body, never at an act. The figure sets the records side by side; it does not say that any
of them caused a report, a post or a statement.

## The figure's own notes

**Reading it:** the dots count pieces, not readers; a piece is placed on the first day a saved Techmeme or Memeorandum page lists it (pages for 9 am, noon, 4 pm, 8 pm and 11 pm Eastern each day), and 9 more pieces seen only on undated pages are left out. A piece counts as on the story when its address or listed headline names Huang, Megaspeed or Alice Huang, tells of the spy affair without naming them, or names Nvidia with a word of the affair (resign, board, inquiry, chief, successor and the like). That rule was applied by script, not by reading each piece, to the 2,465 links on those pages that name Huang, Nvidia, Megaspeed or the spy story. The 231 links the rule left out were then read, 150 of them by an independent auditor and the rest by the builder: 20 are plainly on the story and are counted with it, and 16 could not be settled from their headlines. Left out are 117 links on the wider fallout (export controls, the Commerce secretary, the share price, Anthropic's stock offering) and 94 others; a counted piece may name Huang for another reason. Every link, its class and each reading is published with the figure's tables. They are sorted into the three kinds by site and by the section named in the page address, not by reading them. A chip is one dated act, worded from a saved report; it starts on its day, and a long one may run on over empty days to its right. View counts are those shown when the posts were saved on Nov 12. An act is placed on the day its source gives or, failing that, the day the source was published: those chips have a dashed left edge, and the act itself may be earlier. Where one chip holds two acts of a day and only one is dated by its report, the edge is solid and the words say which. Where a chip rests on one outlet's account of another's report, it says so. Grant figures are awards, not payments traced to a bank. An announced ad is not proof that it ran. Nvidia's board said on Nov 8 it had "no reason to believe" national security or company secrets were compromised, and NBC reported on Nov 12 that Alice Huang's "precise role" remained unclear. Counts of who is named or quoted are counts of articles, each counted once: the 1,086 pages saved by the first search and the 229 saved since (the papers' own articles, saved by hand or in a licensed copy, a few transcripts and a company filing) are 1,236 articles once copies of one article are joined, and 1,154 of them are counted. Two pages are one article when they have the same address or when a reader judged them so: every two pages that share 25 or more seven-word runs making up a fifth of the shorter were opened by a reader from another model family (499 pairs over four readings; six more were decided by the builder under a stated rule, and the table says which. Of the 505, 87 are the same article, as a syndicated or licensed copy with its lead kept, a later version at one address or two snapshots of a live page, and 418 are different, such as a statement and the articles quoting it, or a later dispatch with a new lead; four more could not be read, being block pages that are not counted). Copies that share less than that are not listed, so the number of articles can still be a little high. A page's day is the day of its first publication where the page shows one, and otherwise the day of the version saved, read as a U.S. Eastern day from the page's own stamp (50 pages whose metadata and text carry none, or carry one the printed stamp contradicts, were read in their saved copy). A page is not counted when that day is after Nov 12 (57 pages) or before Oct 29 (13, background pages), when the saved copy is a block or error page (6) or holds nothing at all (2), or when it is a reference page or an image (8). For 25 pages whose first extraction had missed the article, its text was taken again from the saved HTML (four of them a second time, after a reading found paragraphs missing or other lines among them); 58 counted pages are still held only in part (a headline and description, a teaser, an excerpt). The ten people at three or four articles (eight members and two public voices) were each read for whether those articles are separate reports, and all have three. Two senators reach three only through a report saved on Nov 16 that the record had pointed to and the saved pages lacked: one through a piece the aggregators listed on Nov 12 (a paper's live page had repeated its own article, leaving two reports), one through a report that a reader had quoted under a blog post. The saved pages are about half of the pieces listed, so a member whose reports were not saved can be missing from these counts. Members of Congress drawn are every current member named in three or more of those 1,154 articles, as separate reports, for something said or done about the matter in these days, and one senator (Cotton) whom they name that often for a letter on Megaspeed a year earlier; each one's count is in their row. Seven others named that often are not drawn, because the pages name them for other things: a leadership vote, a lost election, a race still being counted, an impeachment bid and other inquiries. The names searched came from the readings of the pages, so on Nov 16 every sitting member's name was also searched as a whole name over the counted articles and over the headlines of the listed pieces that could not be saved (about half of the pieces listed were saved): 53 more members are named, none in more than two. ## Check it yourself

```
python3 scripts/verify_nv8.py
```

recounts the pieces, the sites and the pieces per day from `research/coverage_rows.tsv` and `research/harvest.tsv`, checks
that each piece's day is the day of the capture named for it in `research/coverage_day_pages.tsv`,
recomputes every member's line on employees' gifts from the FEC lines in `research/employee_gifts.csv`, checks that each of
the 2,694 rows the figure rests on has a source a reader can open, and looks up the 787 passages whose source is copied
here. It needs only Python. The other 3,019 passages are a link and the words to find there (`research/evidence.tsv`).

```
pip install playwright pillow && playwright install chromium
python3 scripts/build_nv8.py        # writes figures/nv8-fifteen-days-mapped.html, .png, .jpg
```

rebuilds the figure from the tables in `research/` and the row files in `scripts/`. It measures text with the Liberation
Sans fonts, so it needs them installed to give the same picture.

**One employee's gift.** `research/employee_gifts.csv` has one row per FEC line counted: the committee, the date, the
amount, the employer as filed, and the line's FEC identifiers; `fec_page_url` opens the page of the committee's report
that holds the line. Donors are numbered, not named: the names are on the FEC's pages, and this repository does not
repeat them. Lobbyists are not named either.

direction of the repository's owner. Anthropic is one of the subjects of the figure. For that reason every statement is
