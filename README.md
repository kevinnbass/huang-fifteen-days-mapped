# Fifteen days, mapped: the coverage of the Huang affair, who acted, and the documented ties

**Status: draft.** Every quoted passage has been matched by script to a saved copy of its source, and the counts in the
figure are recomputed from the tables here by `scripts/verify_nv8.py`. A tie is a dated record, not a motive. Nothing
here says why anyone acted.

## What this is

On Oct 29, 2026 The New York Times reported that U.S. officials saw Jensen Huang, the founder and then chief executive of
Nvidia, as the target of a Chinese spy plot. Fifteen days later he had resigned as chief executive and three House
committees had called him to answer questions. This figure puts three things on one sheet for those fifteen days:

- **The coverage.** One dot for each of the 2,245 pieces that two news aggregators, Techmeme and Memeorandum, listed
  on the story, on the first day a saved page lists it (2,254 pieces from 485 sites in all; 9 seen only on undated pages are
  not drawn).
- **Who acted.** One row for each actor (newspapers, reporters, analysts, 27 members of Congress, the administration,
  Nvidia, others) and one chip for each dated act: 362 acts, each worded from a saved report.
- **The documented ties.** At the left, the records that tie those actors to a company, a funder or a group: the AI
  company Anthropic, its people and its PAC; groups that Anthropic or the foundation Open Philanthropy has funded; and
  Nvidia itself. The ties are mapped outward from Anthropic and Open Philanthropy; Nvidia's are added where the same
  kind of record was searched, and the figure's note lists what was searched for it and what was not found. There are 150 lines for the 27 members of Congress (gifts by each company's
  employees, lobbying reports that name a member's bill, fellows and former staff, lobbyists' gifts, ads), a line on
  AnthroPAC's contribution for each member who received one, and 87 records among the other actors.

Why those parties: the matter touches the export of the AI chips that Nvidia sells. On the record here: Anthropic backs
"smart export controls on AI chips" and gave $40 million to the group Public First Action; members of Congress drawn
here sponsor bills on chip exports (one, the AI OVERWATCH Act, would let Congress block advanced chip exports); both
Anthropic's and Nvidia's lobbying reports name those bills; AMD and Broadcom compete with Nvidia for AI chip sales. The
figure's own boxes say, for each group of actors, what is on the record about it and what no document here shows.

A line ends at a person or a body, never at an act. The figure sets the records side by side; it does not say that any
of them caused a report, a post or a statement.

**The interactive map.** `docs/index.html` is the figure as a sheet to pan and zoom, with a panel that opens the record behind whatever is clicked: a chip's passages and links,
the record behind a tie line, every record at a box, a day's acts and headlines, everything that cites a source. GitHub Pages serves it; the link is in this repository's About box.
It is one static file built by `scripts/build_site_nv8.py` from the figure and the tables here, and holds nothing they do not. It is rebuilt after each change to them and says which commit it was built from.
The twelfth independent reading read its first public version: the records, dates, money and line ends agreed with the tables, and it found three errors in what the page showed around them, corrected since (below); the corrected page has been checked by script only.

## The figure's own notes

**Reading it:** the dots count pieces, not readers; a piece is placed on the first day a saved Techmeme or Memeorandum page lists it (pages for 9 am, noon, 4 pm, 8 pm and 11 pm Eastern each day), and 9 more pieces seen only on undated pages are left out. A piece counts as on the story when its address or listed headline names Huang, Megaspeed or Alice Huang, tells of the spy affair without naming them, or names Nvidia with a word of the affair (resign, board, inquiry, chief, successor and the like). That rule was applied by script, not by reading each piece, to the 2,465 links on those pages that name Huang, Nvidia, Megaspeed or the spy story. The 231 links the rule left out were then read, 150 of them by an independent auditor and the rest by the builder: 20 are plainly on the story and are counted with it, and 16 could not be settled from their headlines. Left out are 117 links on the wider fallout (export controls, the Commerce secretary, the share price, Anthropic's stock offering) and 94 others; a counted piece may name Huang for another reason. Every link, its class and each reading is published with the figure's tables. They are sorted into the three kinds by site and by the section named in the page address, not by reading them. A chip is one dated act, worded from a saved report; it starts on its day, and a long one may run on over empty days to its right. View counts are those shown when the posts were saved on Nov 12. An act is placed on the day its source gives: a day the source states, or one its context fixes (a remark dated with others like it, an answer to something that happened that day in a report of that day). Failing that it is placed on the day the source was first published: those chips have a dashed left edge, and the act itself may be earlier. For 15 of the acts so dated a cited page was changed after it was first published, and a passage taken from it (for some, one passage of several) may be newer than the act's day. Where one chip holds two acts of a day and only one is dated by its report, the edge is solid and the words say which. The rule for refusals to comment: an explicit refusal by a drawn actor about its own conduct or part in the matter is drawn once for each documented response, with the answer the same source prints beside it; refusals of one day to several outlets share an act that names each. The refusals found that way are listed with the tables; one that no reader's line records is not seen. A report that no reply had come to a request is not a refusal and is drawn for no one. A department's statements and refusals are drawn on its secretary's row. An act is drawn when a saved report gives it, it is on the matter and no chip of that actor already carries the same occurrence: a similar remark on another occasion is its own act; a later article repeating a dated remark is not. What is drawn was tested with three gauges over the readers' lines from the saved pages (an actor carried on a day with no chip; quoted remarks in two or more articles that no chip holds; the same words on two pages); they find candidates and do not prove completeness: an act reported in paraphrase, one that a single article carries on a day the actor already has a chip, or one under a speaker's name the gauges do not match to a drawn actor may still be missing. Where a chip rests on one outlet's account of another's report, it says so. Grant figures are awards, not payments traced to a bank. An announced ad is not proof that it ran. Nvidia's board said on Nov 8 it had "no reason to believe" national security or company secrets were compromised, and NBC reported on Nov 12 that Alice Huang's "precise role" remained unclear. Counts of who is named or quoted are counts of articles, each counted once: the 1,086 pages saved by the first search and the 229 saved since (the papers' own articles, saved by hand or in a licensed copy, a few transcripts and a company filing) are 1,236 articles once copies of one article are joined, and 1,154 of them are counted. Two pages are one article when they have the same address or when a reader judged them so: every two pages that share 25 or more seven-word runs making up a fifth of the shorter were opened by a reader from another model family (504 pairs over six readings; six more were decided by the builder under a stated rule, and the table says which. Of the 510, 87 are the same article, as a syndicated or licensed copy with its lead kept, a later version at one address or two snapshots of a live page, and 423 are different, such as a statement and the articles quoting it, or a later dispatch with a new lead; four more could not be read, being block pages that are not counted). Copies that share less than that are not listed, so the number of articles can still be a little high. A page's day is the day of its first publication where the page shows one, and otherwise the day of the version saved, read as a U.S. Eastern day from the page's own stamp (50 pages whose metadata and text carry none, or carry one the printed stamp contradicts, were read in their saved copy). A page is not counted when that day is after Nov 12 (57 pages) or before Oct 29 (13, background pages), when the saved copy is a block or error page (6) or holds nothing at all (2), or when it is a reference page or an image (8). For 25 pages whose first extraction had missed the article, its text was taken again from the saved HTML (four of them a second time, after a reading found paragraphs missing or other lines among them); 98 counted pages are still held only in part (a headline and description, a teaser, an excerpt, the opening before a subscription barrier, a live page whose entries are collapsed or load only on request): 58 that the readers of the pages had listed, and 40 more found on Nov 16: 35 among the 248 counted pages that show a sign of a barrier or hold a short text, which readers went through, and five live pages that later readings found among the longer pages. A page is whole when all of the listed page's own piece is saved, whatever it quotes or links to. A longer page that shows no sign of a barrier was not read again for this: every counted page with a collapse mark in its text was read, and every saved page whose HTML holds a live-blog record was compared with its text; a live page saved by hand as PDF cannot be checked that way. On three live pages the saved HTML held 172 entries that the text read lacked, and they were added to it. One of them is a four-day live page of general news: an article is counted for a member when its text names the member anywhere, so there, as in any round-up, the name can stand in an entry about something else; the four members it names only for other news carry a note beside their count. On six pages a table or a photo caption of the piece, which the extraction from the saved HTML had left out, was added to the text read, after a search of every page's HTML for a member's name missing from its text (a member counts as named when the name stands in the piece's own table or caption), and on three pages a short paragraph or passage; other photo captions and some small parts of a piece (a subheading, a credit, a short sentence) are not in the text read, and that search does not find other passages an extraction may have lost. On four pages saved by hand as PDF, 21 lines of prose that the cleaning of the text had dropped at page breaks were put back. The ten people at three or four articles (eight members and two public voices) were each read for whether those articles are separate reports, and all have three. Two senators reach three only through a report saved on Nov 16 that the record had pointed to and the saved pages lacked: one through a piece the aggregators listed on Nov 12 (a paper's live page had repeated its own article, leaving two reports), one through a report that a reader had quoted under a blog post. The saved pages are about half of the pieces listed, so a member whose reports were not saved can be missing from these counts. Members of Congress drawn are every current member named in three or more of those 1,154 articles, as separate reports, for something said or done about the matter in these days, and one senator (Cotton) whom they name that often for a letter on Megaspeed a year earlier; each one's count is in their row. Ten others named that often are not drawn, because the pages name them for other things: a leadership vote, a lost election, a race still being counted, an impeachment bid, a missed vote and other inquiries. The names searched came from the readings of the pages, so on Nov 16 every sitting member's name was also searched as a whole name over the counted articles and over the headlines of the listed pieces that could not be saved (about half of the pieces listed were saved): 60 more members are named, none in more than two. The count for every name searched is published with the figure's tables. The articles counted quote 280 analysts, experts and research groups (a person or firm written two ways is counted once). Each was looked up in the records held. On Nov 15, and on Nov 16 for the names added since, the names not drawn were searched again as whole names in the 85 tables of the four evidence sets: 1,022 names of two to four words among the analysts and the bylines (people's names, and a few firms and labels of the same form), and 36 other firm names and one-word names among the analysts; 81 entries (bylines that are an agency, a label or an address, and analyst labels under four letters) were left out of that pass. The 16 drawn each have a remark of these days and a tie on record to Anthropic, Open Philanthropy or Nvidia: their own (a fellowship, research support, a past post, a seat on an advisory council) or that of the organisation where they hold a post (an award to it, a paid business relationship, a paper it was commissioned to write); each row says which, and an award to an organisation is not a payment to the person. At a university an award counts for a person only where the record names that person's own unit. Not drawn: 4 people at universities whose only award on record names the university as a whole, or another researcher there; 8 for whom the record is a remark or report not shown to be of these days, a past post at a group funded only later, a grant from another company, a grant to their group from an investor in Anthropic, or an invitation to speak with no post or payment on record; 2 the look-up could not tell apart from others of the same name; 17 entries that are one of the banks below or its analysts; and the other 233, for whom no record was found. No tie is implied for anyone not drawn. Bank analysts, and investors quoted for their view of the shares, are not drawn, whatever their ties; three investors, Ackman, Maguire and Conway, are drawn among the public voices, under that row's rule of three articles. Quartz reported on Sep 4, 2026, citing unnamed sources of Bloomberg's, that Anthropic was nearing a credit facility before its share sale, and named 17 banks in it, four of them also leading the sale; the report says the details could still change. Analysts, or executives in office, of 11 of those banks are quoted on the saved pages. The banks' business with Nvidia was not looked up. Two quoted investors are described on the pages as a shareholder and a short seller of Nvidia. The pages counted carry 948 bylines, counting a name once for each outlet it appears at: 873 different names, 75 of them agencies or not a person's name. These are the bylines of the pages counted, those of the first search and those saved since; a page saved as an actor's own original is cited on that actor's chip and its byline is not counted here. Each was looked up in the fellowship and staff records held and, on Nov 15 or 16, in the whole-name search. Five have a tie on record and are drawn: Perlo and Hashim from the records held, Kubinec from his own bio at The Dispatch, Mowshowitz and Schneider from pages saved on Nov 15 (the first look-up had missed both). Two more have a record of another kind and no tie is drawn: a congressional fellow of the 2021 cohort of a programme that Open Philanthropy funded in 2025 (the record shows no overlap), and a reporter named on the page of a CSET event. A CSET staff member's byline on a CSET release of 2025 is no longer counted, because that page is dated before these days (he also wrote CSET's two releases of Nov 10 and 11, which are drawn in CSET's row). For three the tables hold the name and nothing ties it to the reporter. Pages that could not be saved were not checked. The lists of the names looked up are not published, because most name people for whom nothing was found; the counts and the rules are. "No AnthroPAC contribution on file" means none in the PAC's filings with the Federal Election Commission for Jun 29 to Sep 30, 2026. A line that says no gift from a company's employees means that no FEC line for Jan 2025 to Oct 14, 2026 gives that company as the donor's employer to a committee tied to the member; it is not proof that no employee gave. Lines dated after Oct 14, 2026 are not in the FEC records read here: where a committee must file a post-general report (its books close Nov 23), that report is due on Dec 3, 2026; other committees follow their own schedules. No line of a sitting member's campaign committee dated after Sep 30, 2026 is on file yet from either company's employees. Gifts made through the conduits ActBlue and WinRed after that day are on file only as the conduits' own lines (26 from people who gave Anthropic as employer, $2,741; 350 for Nvidia, $14,345) and are not counted until the recipient's own report shows them; among the members drawn they name only 2 Nvidia lines ($15) for the committee of Sen. Elizabeth Warren. A committee is tied to a member when it is the member's campaign committee, a leadership PAC the member sponsors, or a joint fundraising committee whose statement of organisation lists that member's committee and no other sitting member's; money given to a joint committee is shared with its other participants (a party committee, a PAC), and the FEC lines do not show how much reached the member's own campaign. Joint committees of several members are listed with the figure's tables and attributed to no one. An employee's gift is counted from an FEC line on which the donor gave the company as employer, to a committee tied to the member; donors are counted, not named, and the two companies are counted by one rule, though not from equally deep records, as the next sentences say. Both companies' lines come from the FEC's bulk file of itemised individual contributions for the 2026 cycle, downloaded Nov 13, 2026 (it carries whole dollars), plus the lines its API holds and the file does not yet (nearly all dated Jul to Sep 2026): 80 for Anthropic, 21 for Nvidia. For 8 more lines the API's later filing replaces the file's copy. Nine Anthropic lines to Banks for Senate are counted from a copy of the committee's report saved in Sep 2026: its amended report leaves the employer blank. No earlier copy exists for Nvidia, so a Nvidia line blanked that way is missed. Sums are of positive receipt lines, in whole dollars as the FEC's bulk file gives them. Negative lines on the same committees (refunds and chargebacks: 3 for Anthropic, $4,287; 4 for Nvidia, $200) are listed with the tables and not taken off; among the members drawn, $3,500 returned by a committee of Sen. John Cornyn. A person is counted by surname and first given name; in six of the counts a name occurs with a second spelling or ZIP code, which shows neither two people nor one, and the count stays as the rule gives it. A lobbying report names a bill; it does not say which office was visited or what was asked. Fellows, advisers and former staff are as the programme's, the group's or Congress's own pages list them. A lobbyists' line names the clients that the lobbying reports held here list for the givers' firms. The reports give each gift as the lobbyist's own money; they do not show whether a client asked for it. Whether the firm had the client when the gift was made is taken from the firm's registration dates, which the published table gives beside each gift. Why each client is on this map: Open Philanthropy's awards on record include $3.4M to Americans for Responsible Innovation (2024–25), $12.5M to the Center for AI Safety (2022–23) and $1.9M to the Future of Life Institute (2015–20), and its action fund gave IFP Action $2.0M (2022–23). Nvidia registered its own political committee on Aug 27, 2026; its one report on file, through Sep 30, shows no contribution to any candidate. The same record was then pulled for both companies: the lobbyists' contribution reports for 2020 to 2026 of all 33 firms registered for Nvidia, for Anthropic or for one of the groups above. To the 27 members drawn, in gifts of Feb 2021 to Jun 2026 made while the firm was registered for the client, lobbyists listed for the client gave $70,469 on Nvidia's side, $78,500 on Anthropic's and $85,491 on the groups' side; counting every lobbyist at those firms, $429,735, $124,500 and $295,629. A firm registered for two clients puts one gift on two sides, so the sides are not added up. Each member's row gives its lines from this table by the same rule for each side: every gift of a lobbyist at a firm then registered for the client, and how much of it came from lobbyists listed for that client. Only a lobbyist's own money is counted. The reports also list contributions of political committees (a firm's PAC, a PAC a lobbyist serves, a campaign committee): 55 such lines, 28 gifts once those listed by several lobbyists of one firm are counted once, are in the table and in no sum here; until Nov 16, 2026 they were counted as lobbyists' own money. **What the record does not show:** who gave the intelligence assessment to reporters; whether it is correct; any contact about these reports between Anthropic, or anyone tied to it, and the reporters or their sources; any contact with a member of Congress about these reports or about what the member then said or did (earlier public contacts, such as testimony at a hearing, are drawn at left). The saved reports state limits of their own: the Times's first report says officials "did not believe" Huang knew the woman was gathering information when the president was first briefed, and that Huang "has not received a defensive briefing"; Nvidia said its customers get "zero information relevant to national security", and the Times reports its spokesman saying that customers do not ask Nvidia to press the U.S. government to change its policies on chip sales to China. That first report also says it "could not be determined which intelligence agency or agencies" monitored her, and that it is not clear what information she passed along or what it was worth to China. NBC reports that the president, asked whether he was briefed before he took Huang to China in May, said "I didn't hear about it then" and later "They could have been mentioned."

## Check it yourself

```
python3 scripts/verify_nv8.py
```

recounts the pieces, the sites and the pieces per day from `research/coverage_rows.tsv` and `research/harvest.tsv`, checks
that each piece's day is the day of the capture named for it in `research/coverage_day_pages.tsv`,
recomputes every member's line on employees' gifts from the FEC lines in `research/employee_gifts.csv`, checks that each of
the 2,894 rows the figure rests on has a source a reader can open, and looks up the 788 passages whose source is copied
here. It needs only Python. The other 3,818 passages are a link and the words to find there (`research/evidence.tsv`).

```
pip install playwright pillow && playwright install chromium
python3 scripts/build_nv8.py        # writes figures/nv8-fifteen-days-mapped.html, .png, .jpg
```

rebuilds the figure from the tables in `research/` and the row files in `scripts/`. It measures text with the Liberation
Sans fonts, so it needs them installed to give the same picture.

**One employee's gift.** `research/employee_gifts.csv` has one row per FEC line counted: the committee, the date, the
amount, the employer as filed, and the line's FEC identifiers; `fec_page_url` opens the page of the committee's report
that holds the line. Donors are numbered, not named: the names are on the FEC's pages, and this repository does not
repeat them. Lobbyists are not named either.

## Who made this

The figure, the tables, the scripts and this file were drafted with Claude, a model made by Anthropic, working at the
direction of the repository's owner. Anthropic is one of the subjects of the figure. For that reason every statement is
tied to a record a reader can open, donors and lobbyists are counted and not named, and the work was audited by another
company's model.
