# Fifteen days, mapped: the coverage of the Huang affair, who acted, and the documented ties

**Status: draft.** Every quoted passage has been matched by script to a saved copy of its source, and the counts in the
figure are recomputed from the tables here by `scripts/verify_nv8.py`. A tie is a dated record, not a motive. Nothing
here says why anyone acted.

## What this is

On Oct 29, 2026 The New York Times reported that U.S. officials saw Jensen Huang, the founder and then chief executive of
Nvidia, as the target of a Chinese spy plot. Fifteen days later he had resigned as chief executive and three House
committees had called him to answer questions. This figure puts three things on one sheet for those fifteen days:

- **The coverage.** One dot for each of the 2,245 pieces that two news aggregators, Techmeme and Memeorandum, listed
  on the story, on the first day a saved page lists it (2,254 pieces from 485 sites in all; 9 seen only on undated pages are
  not drawn).
- **Who acted.** One row for each actor (newspapers, reporters, analysts, 27 members of Congress, the administration,
  Nvidia, others) and one chip for each dated act: 163 acts, each worded from a saved report.
- **The documented ties.** At the left, the records that tie those actors to a company, a funder or a group: the AI
  company Anthropic, its people and its PAC; groups that Anthropic or the foundation Open Philanthropy has funded; and
  Nvidia itself. The ties are mapped outward from Anthropic and Open Philanthropy; Nvidia's are added where the same
  kind of record was searched, and the figure's note lists what was searched for it and what was not found. There are 150 lines for the 27 members of Congress (gifts by each company's
  employees, lobbying reports that name a member's bill, fellows and former staff, lobbyists' gifts, ads), a line on
  AnthroPAC's contribution for each member who received one, and 84 records among the other actors.

Why those parties: the matter touches the export of the AI chips that Nvidia sells. On the record here: Anthropic backs
"smart export controls on AI chips" and gave $40 million to the group Public First Action; members of Congress drawn
here sponsor bills on chip exports (one, the AI OVERWATCH Act, would let Congress block advanced chip exports); both
Anthropic's and Nvidia's lobbying reports name those bills; AMD and Broadcom compete with Nvidia for AI chip sales. The
figure's own boxes say, for each group of actors, what is on the record about it and what no document here shows.

A line ends at a person or a body, never at an act. The figure sets the records side by side; it does not say that any
of them caused a report, a post or a statement.

## The figure's own notes

**Reading it:** the dots count pieces, not readers; a piece is placed on the first day a saved Techmeme or Memeorandum page lists it (pages for 9 am, noon, 4 pm, 8 pm and 11 pm Eastern each day), and 9 more pieces seen only on undated pages are left out. A piece counts as on the story when its address or listed headline names Huang, Megaspeed or Alice Huang, tells of the spy affair without naming them, or names Nvidia with a word of the affair (resign, board, inquiry, chief, successor and the like). That rule was applied by script, not by reading each piece, to the 2,465 links on those pages that name Huang, Nvidia, Megaspeed or the spy story. The 231 links the rule left out were then read, 150 of them by an independent auditor and the rest by the builder: 20 are plainly on the story and are counted with it, and 16 could not be settled from their headlines. Left out are 117 links on the wider fallout (export controls, the Commerce secretary, the share price, Anthropic's stock offering) and 94 others; a counted piece may name Huang for another reason. Every link, its class and each reading is published with the figure's tables. They are sorted into the three kinds by site and by the section named in the page address, not by reading them. A chip is one dated act, worded from a saved report; it starts on its day, and a long one may run on over empty days to its right. View counts are those shown when the posts were saved on Nov 12. An act is placed on the day its source gives or, failing that, the day the source was published: those chips have a dashed left edge, and the act itself may be earlier. Where one chip holds two acts of a day and only one is dated by its report, the edge is solid and the words say which. Where a chip rests on one outlet's account of another's report, it says so. Grant figures are awards, not payments traced to a bank. An announced ad is not proof that it ran. Nvidia's board said on Nov 8 it had "no reason to believe" national security or company secrets were compromised, and NBC reported on Nov 12 that Alice Huang's "precise role" remained unclear. Counts of who is named or quoted are counts of articles, each counted once: the 1,086 pages saved by the first search and the 173 saved since (the papers' own articles, saved by hand or in a licensed copy, and a few transcripts) are 1,201 articles once copies of one article are joined (the same address, or four fifths of the shorter text inside the longer), and 1,134 of them are counted. A page is not counted when its own date, read as a U.S. Eastern day, is after Nov 12 (44 pages) or before Oct 29 (12, background pages), when no text could be read (9) or when it is not a news piece (7); 62 pages carry no date and are counted. A republished copy dated later than its original is counted only through a counted copy of the same article. Members of Congress drawn are every current member named in three or more of those 1,134 articles for something said or done about the matter in these days, and one senator (Cotton) whom they name that often for a letter on Megaspeed a year earlier; each one's count is in their row. ## Check it yourself

```
python3 scripts/verify_nv8.py
```

recounts the pieces, the sites and the pieces per day from `research/coverage_rows.tsv` and `research/harvest.tsv`, checks
that each piece's day is the day of the capture named for it in `research/coverage_day_pages.tsv`,
recomputes every member's line on employees' gifts from the FEC lines in `research/employee_gifts.csv`, checks that each of
the 2,692 rows the figure rests on has a source a reader can open, and looks up the 835 passages whose source is copied
here. It needs only Python. The other 3,003 passages are a link and the words to find there (`research/evidence.tsv`).

```
pip install playwright pillow && playwright install chromium
python3 scripts/build_nv8.py        # writes figures/nv8-fifteen-days-mapped.html, .png, .jpg
```

rebuilds the figure from the tables in `research/` and the row files in `scripts/`. It measures text with the Liberation
Sans fonts, so it needs them installed to give the same picture.

**One employee's gift.** `research/employee_gifts.csv` has one row per FEC line counted: the committee, the date, the
amount, the employer as filed, and the line's FEC identifiers; `fec_page_url` opens the page of the committee's report
that holds the line. Donors are numbered, not named: the names are on the FEC's pages, and this repository does not
repeat them. Lobbyists are not named either.

| `sources/` | copies of sources that are official records or the builder's own captures |
| `audit/` | the audit's brief, report, findings and recounts, and the disposition of every finding |
| `scripts/` | `build_nv8.py` and `verify_nv8.py` run on this copy, with the row files the figure is drawn from (`rows_ties.py` and `rows_events.py` are shared with other figures of the same project and also hold their rows); `as_built/` holds scripts as they were run, which need the builder's evidence sets and downloads |

News reports, the companies' and other organisations' pages and posts are linked and quoted in a sentence; they are not
copied. Headlines are given as the aggregators listed them.
