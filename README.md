# Fifteen days, mapped: the coverage of the Huang affair, who acted, and the documented ties

**Status: draft.** Every quoted passage has been matched by script to a saved copy of its source, and the counts in the
figure are recomputed from the tables here by `scripts/verify_nv8.py`. A tie is a dated record, not a motive. Nothing
here says why anyone acted.

## What this is

On Oct 29, 2026 The New York Times reported that U.S. officials saw Jensen Huang, the founder and then chief executive of
Nvidia, as the target of a Chinese spy plot. Fifteen days later he had resigned as chief executive and three House
committees had called him to answer questions. This figure puts three things on one sheet for those fifteen days:

- **The coverage.** One dot for each of the 2,245 pieces that two news aggregators, Techmeme and Memeorandum, listed
  on the story, on the first day a saved page lists it (2,254 pieces from 485 sites in all; 9 seen only on undated pages are
  not drawn).
- **Who acted.** One row for each actor (newspapers, reporters, analysts, 24 members of Congress, the administration,
  Nvidia, others) and one chip for each dated act: 150 acts, each worded from a saved report.
- **The documented ties.** At the left, the records that tie those actors to a company, a funder or a group: the AI
  company Anthropic, its people and its PAC; groups that Anthropic or the foundation Open Philanthropy has funded; and
  Nvidia itself. The ties are mapped outward from Anthropic and Open Philanthropy; Nvidia's are added where the same
  kind of record was searched, and the figure's note lists what was searched for it and what was not found. There are 115 lines for the 24 members of Congress (gifts by each company's
  employees, lobbying reports that name a member's bill, fellows and former staff, lobbyists' gifts, ads), a line on
  AnthroPAC's contribution for each member who received one, and 81 records among the other actors.

Why those parties: the matter touches the export of the AI chips that Nvidia sells. On the record here: Anthropic backs
"smart export controls on AI chips" and gave $40 million to the group Public First Action; members of Congress drawn
here sponsor bills on chip exports (one, the AI OVERWATCH Act, would let Congress block advanced chip exports); both
Anthropic's and Nvidia's lobbying reports name those bills; AMD and Broadcom compete with Nvidia for AI chip sales. The
figure's own boxes say, for each group of actors, what is on the record about it and what no document here shows.

A line ends at a person or a body, never at an act. The figure sets the records side by side; it does not say that any
of them caused a report, a post or a statement.

## The figure's own notes

**Reading it:** the dots count pieces, not readers; a piece is placed on the first day a saved Techmeme or Memeorandum page lists it (pages for 9 am, noon, 4 pm, 8 pm and 11 pm Eastern each day), and 9 more pieces seen only on undated pages are left out. A piece counts as on the story when its address or listed headline names Huang, Megaspeed or Alice Huang, tells of the spy affair without naming them, or names Nvidia with a word of the affair (resign, board, inquiry, chief, successor and the like). That rule was applied by script, not by reading each piece, to the 2,465 links on those pages that name Huang, Nvidia, Megaspeed or the spy story. The 231 links the rule left out were then read, 150 of them by an independent auditor and the rest by the builder: 20 are plainly on the story and are counted with it, and 16 could not be settled from their headlines. Left out are 117 links on the wider fallout (export controls, the Commerce secretary, the share price, Anthropic's stock offering) and 94 others; a counted piece may name Huang for another reason. Every link, its class and each reading is published with the figure's tables. They are sorted into the three kinds by site and by the section named in the page address, not by reading them. A chip is one dated act, worded from a saved report; it starts on its day, and a long one may run on over empty days to its right. View counts are those shown when the posts were saved on Nov 12. An act is placed on the day its source gives or, failing that, the day the source was published: those chips have a dashed left edge, and the act itself may be earlier. Where a chip rests on one outlet's account of another's report, it says so. Grant figures are awards, not payments traced to a bank. An announced ad is not proof that it ran. Nvidia's board said on Nov 8 it had "no reason to believe" national security or company secrets were compromised, and NBC reported on Nov 12 that Alice Huang's "precise role" remained unclear. Members of Congress drawn are every current member named on three or more of the 1,086 saved pages for something said or done about the matter in these days, and one senator (Cotton) whom the pages name that often for a letter on Megaspeed a year earlier; each one's count is in their row. Six others named that often are not drawn, because the pages name them for other things: a leadership vote, a lost election, an impeachment bid and other inquiries. The count for every name searched is published with the figure's tables. The saved pages quote 240 analysts, experts and research groups (a person or firm written two ways is counted once). Each was looked up in the records held. On Nov 15 the names not drawn were searched again as whole names in the 85 tables of the four evidence sets: 797 people's names of two to four words among the analysts and the bylines, and 34 firm names and one-word names among the analysts; 69 entries that are an agency, a label or an address were left out of that pass. The 15 drawn each have a remark of these days and a tie on record to Anthropic, Open Philanthropy or Nvidia: their own (a fellowship, research support, a past post) or that of the organisation where they hold a post (an award to it, a paid business relationship, a paper it was commissioned to write); each row says which, and an award to an organisation is not a payment to the person. At a university an award counts for a person only where the record names that person's own unit. Not drawn: 3 people at universities whose only award on record names the university as a whole, or another researcher there; 7 for whom the record is a remark or report not shown to be of these days, a past post at a group funded only later, a grant from another company, or an invitation to speak with no post or payment on record; 1 the look-up could not tell apart from another person of the same name; 13 entries that are one of the banks below or its analysts; and the other 201, for whom no record was found. No tie is implied for anyone not drawn. Bank analysts, and investors quoted for their view of the shares, are not drawn, whatever their ties; two investors, Ackman and Maguire, are drawn among the public voices, under that row's rule of three pages. Quartz reported on Sep 4, 2026, citing unnamed sources of Bloomberg's, that Anthropic was nearing a credit facility before its share sale, and named 17 banks in it, four of them also leading the sale; the report says the details could still change. Analysts or executives of 10 of those banks are quoted on the saved pages. The banks' business with Nvidia was not looked up. Two quoted investors are described on the pages as a shareholder and a short seller of Nvidia. The pages searched carry 729 bylines, counting a name once for each outlet it appears at: 677 different names, 67 of them agencies or not a person's name. These are the bylines of the 1,086 pages searched; a page saved later as an actor's own original is cited on that actor's chip and its byline is not counted here. Each was looked up in the fellowship and staff records held and, on Nov 15, in the whole-name search. Five have a tie on record and are drawn: Perlo and Hashim from the records held, Kubinec from his own bio at The Dispatch, Mowshowitz and Schneider from pages saved on Nov 15 (the first look-up had missed both). Three more have a record of another kind and no tie is drawn: a congressional fellow of the 2021 cohort of a programme that Open Philanthropy funded in 2025 (the record shows no overlap); a reporter named on the page of a CSET event; and a CSET staff member whose counted byline is on a CSET release of 2025 (he also wrote CSET's two releases of Nov 10 and 11, which are drawn in CSET's row). For two the tables hold the name and nothing ties it to the reporter. Pages that could not be saved were not checked. The lists of the names looked up are not published, because most name people for whom nothing was found; the counts and the rules are. "No AnthroPAC contribution on file" means none in the PAC's filings with the Federal Election Commission for Jun 29 to Sep 30, 2026. A line that says no gift from a company's employees means that no FEC line for Jan 2025 to Oct 14, 2026 gives that company as the donor's employer to a committee tied to the member; it is not proof that no employee gave. Elizabeth Warren. Joint committees of several members are listed with the figure's tables and attributed to no one. An employee's gift is counted from an FEC line on which the donor gave the company as employer, to a committee tied to the member; donors are counted, not named, and the two companies are counted by one rule, though not from equally deep records, as the next sentences say. A lobbyists' line names the clients that the lobbying reports held here list for the givers' firms. The reports give each gift as the lobbyist's own money; they do not show whether a client asked for it, or whether the firm had that client when the gift was made. Why each client is on this map: Open Philanthropy's awards on record include $3.4M to Americans for Responsible Innovation (2024–25), $12.5M to the Center for AI Safety (2022–23) and $1.9M to the Future of Life Institute (2015–20), and its action fund gave IFP Action $2.0M (2022–23). To the 24 members drawn, in gifts of Feb 2021 to Jun 2026 made while the firm was registered for the client, lobbyists listed for the client gave $67,969 on Nvidia's side, $71,000 on Anthropic's and $80,491 on the groups' side; counting every lobbyist at those firms, $391,735, $109,000 and $340,079. **What the record does not show:** who gave the intelligence assessment to reporters; whether it is correct; any contact about these reports between Anthropic, or anyone tied to it, and the reporters or their sources; any contact with a member of Congress about these reports or about what the member then said or did (earlier public contacts, such as testimony at a hearing, are drawn at left). ## Check it yourself

```
python3 scripts/verify_nv8.py
```

recounts the pieces, the sites and the pieces per day from `research/coverage_rows.tsv` and `research/harvest.tsv`, checks
that each piece's day is the day of the capture named for it in `research/coverage_day_pages.tsv`,
recomputes every member's line on employees' gifts from the FEC lines in `research/employee_gifts.csv`, checks that each of
the 2,641 rows the figure rests on has a source a reader can open, and looks up the 485 passages whose source is copied
here. It needs only Python. The other 2,967 passages are a link and the words to find there (`research/evidence.tsv`).

```
pip install playwright pillow && playwright install chromium
python3 scripts/build_nv8.py        # writes figures/nv8-fifteen-days-mapped.html, .png, .jpg
```

rebuilds the figure from the tables in `research/` and the row files in `scripts/`. It measures text with the Liberation
Sans fonts, so it needs them installed to give the same picture.

**One employee's gift.** `research/employee_gifts.csv` has one row per FEC line counted: the committee, the date, the
amount, the employer as filed, and the line's FEC identifiers; `fec_page_url` opens the page of the committee's report
that holds the line. Donors are numbered, not named: the names are on the FEC's pages, and this repository does not
repeat them. Lobbyists are not named either.

direction of the repository's owner. Anthropic is one of the subjects of the figure. For that reason every statement is
